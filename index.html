01 About

Studying mammals where forest meets farmland

I'm a mammalian conservation ecologist. Most of the world's wild mammals now live in landscapes shaped by agriculture and development, and my research asks what it takes for them to persist there. I combine field methods (live-capture, GPS telemetry and camera trapping) with physiological and toxicological sampling to link how animals use a landscape to the health consequences of living in it.

For my Ph.D. at Cardiff University, I was based for most of each year at Danau Girang Field Centre in the Lower Kinabatangan floodplain of Sabah, Malaysian Borneo. There I led the first GPS satellite-collaring of a viverrid, following Malay and common palm civets through a mosaic of forest fragments and oil palm plantations. I paired their movements with diet, blood profiles and a non-invasive protocol for monitoring heavy metal and agrochemical exposure.

At the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission, I contribute to long-term wildlife population monitoring and to wildlife disease investigations, including the response to highly pathogenic H5N1 avian influenza. That work keeps my research tied to the questions wildlife managers need answered.

02 Research

Four threads, one question

How do mammals persist in human-modified landscapes, and what does it cost them?

  1. Movement & space use in fragmented landscapes

    GPS telemetry and camera traps reveal how carnivores navigate forest fragments, plantations and edges, and which landscape features keep populations connected.

    Malay civets · Sunda clouded leopards · oil palm mosaics

  2. Wildlife health, physiology & ecotoxicology

    Blood profiles and metal and agrochemical exposure in wild carnivores show the physiological cost of living alongside agriculture.

    Indicator species · haematology · heavy metals

  3. Emerging disease & long-term monitoring

    Agency-scale monitoring and disease surveillance, from decades of demographic data to the impacts of H5N1 avian influenza on wildlife.

    HPAI H5N1 · population trends · management

  4. Natural history of understudied mammals

    Baseline ecology for species we still know little about: first activity patterns, new localities and range information that conservation planning depends on.

    Otter civet · Sunda stink-badger · smooth-coated otter
