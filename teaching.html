Teaching

Teaching and mentoring

I teach with an emphasis on fieldwork and hands-on experience, and I enjoy helping students develop their own research projects.

01 Philosophy

Approach

I think mammalogy is best taught as a hands-on course. By the end, students should be able to identify local species, design a survey, handle animals safely and ethically, and analyse field data. I'd build the course around specimen labs for identification and anatomy, field labs using live traps, camera traps and telemetry, and projects using real monitoring data.

Most of my teaching so far has been in the field. As a core field advisor at Danau Girang Field Centre from 2013 to 2019, I trained more than 100 students, volunteers and visiting scientists in tropical forest research techniques. I also coordinated more than 50 university field courses from 16 institutions, involving over 800 students. Before that, I designed and led inquiry-based science programs for school-age students at the W.K. Kellogg Biological Station. At Miami University I helped develop courses for Project Dragonfly's graduate programs in conservation and inquiry-based learning.

02 Courses

Courses I can teach

Undergraduate · Field

Mammalogy

Evolution, diversity, identification and ecology of mammals, with specimen labs and field sessions using live-trapping, camera traps and sign surveys.

Undergraduate · Field

Wildlife Field Techniques

Capture and handling, telemetry, camera-trap study design and the ethics and safety of working with wild animals.

Graduate

Carnivores in Human-Modified Landscapes

A seminar on movement, health and conflict for carnivores in agricultural and urbanising systems, built around primary literature and student-led analyses.

Graduate

Wildlife Health & Ecotoxicology

Contaminants, physiology and emerging disease in wild populations, and how individual health affects populations.

03 Mentoring

Mentoring

From 2016 to 2018 I was academic supervisor to a Malaysian master's student at Universiti Malaysia Sabah. I helped secure her funding and oversaw the design of the first otter survey in Borneo. She defended her thesis on smooth-coated otter ecology, and the work was published in the IUCN Otter Specialist Group Bulletin in 2020. I was also primary field supervisor for four Cardiff University undergraduates on their professional training year research projects, and mentored 22 more.

I encourage students to take ownership of their projects, from developing the question through to publication. I'm particularly keen to give undergraduates their first experience of field research.

04 Outreach

Public engagement

I presented small carnivore research in the Borneo Jungle Diaries television series (Season 1, “Civets: Aromatic Animals”, Scubazoo). I've also presented our Kinabatangan work to Sabah government officials, to two U.S. Ambassadors to Malaysia, and at community open days run with the local ecotourism cooperative, KOPEL.

As an undergraduate, I founded the Miami University Conservation Team to connect students with local and international conservation projects.
