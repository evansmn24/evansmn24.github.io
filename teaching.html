01 Philosophy

Approach

I think mammalogy is best taught as a hands-on course. By the end, students should be able to identify local species, design a survey, handle animals safely and ethically, and analyse field data. I'd build the course around specimen labs for identification and anatomy, field labs using live traps, camera traps and telemetry, and projects using real monitoring data.

Most of what I know about teaching I learned in the field, working with students who had often never been in a rainforest before. Within a few days they could set a camera trap, find their way with a GPS and collect data they would go on to analyse themselves. I want students to leave a course with skills they will actually use in a job, and with a sense of how research feeds into management decisions.

02 Field teaching

Field courses

From 2013 to 2019 I was a core field advisor at Danau Girang Field Centre in Sabah and co-led the Centre's work with visiting university courses. I coordinated more than 50 field courses from 16 institutions, involving over 800 undergraduate and graduate students, and taught on them in the forest, on the river and in the lab.

Visiting courses came from universities including Cardiff, Miami University, Oregon State, Portland State, the University of Hong Kong and Leiden, along with school groups from Malaysia and Singapore.

  • Camera trappingSurvey design, setting stations, species identification and activity data
  • Capture & trackingSmall mammal live-trapping, handling, and GPS, UHF and VHF telemetry
  • Habitat assessmentVegetation plots, forest structure and riparian surveys
  • BehaviourEthograms and focal follows
  • Navigation & safetyGPS navigation, river work and fieldwork in remote conditions
  • AnalysisTurning field data into results in R and GIS

05 Mentoring

Mentoring

I encourage students to take ownership of their projects, from developing the question through to publication. I'm particularly keen to give undergraduates their first experience of field research.

  • M.Sc. student, Universiti Malaysia SabahAcademic supervisor, 2016–2018. I helped secure her funding and oversaw the first otter survey in Borneo. Her thesis on smooth-coated otter ecology was published in the IUCN Otter Specialist Group Bulletin (Wai et al. 2020).
  • Cardiff University placement studentsPrimary field supervisor for four undergraduates on professional training year research projects, and mentor to 22 more, 2013–2019.
  • Field assistants & volunteersTrained local and international assistants, volunteers and visiting scientists in capture, tracking and camera-trap methods on the Kinabatangan Small Carnivore Project.

06 Outreach

Public engagement

I presented small carnivore research in the Borneo Jungle Diaries television series (Season 1, “Civets: Aromatic Animals”, Scubazoo). I've also presented our Kinabatangan work to Sabah government officials, to two U.S. Ambassadors to Malaysia, and at community open days run with the local ecotourism cooperative, KOPEL.

As an undergraduate, I founded the Miami University Conservation Team to connect students with local and international conservation projects.
